This page is for players, not integrators. It explains what you see and click
when you open a session, whether you started it or joined someone else's. For
how the room talks to the broker under the hood, see [Users and invites](/docs/api/join).

## Opening the room

You get to the room one of two ways:

- **You started the session.** RomM opens it for you directly. You are the
  **host**.
- **Someone invited you**, either with a link they copied from their own room,
  or by adding you by name from RomM. Opening that link or URL drops you into
  the same room, alongside the host.

The first time a link drops you into a room with no name attached, you're
asked to pick a username before you can do anything else. It's remembered in
your browser, so you won't be asked again on that device.

<Callout type="info">
If the link says the session isn't running, or is no longer valid, someone
already ended it or it hasn't started yet. Links only work while a session is
live.
</Callout>

## Host, participant, or spectator

Everyone in the room has one of three levels of access:

- **Host** - whoever's game this is. Only the host can invite people, change
  who has a controller, and end the session for everyone.
- **Participant** - joined with a "Collaboration User" invite. Can chat, share
  webcam and mic, and be handed a controller or the mouse and keyboard by the
  host.
- **Spectator** (read-only) - joined with a "Read Only" invite. Can watch and
  chat, but has no webcam/mic controls and can never be handed input.

If you're the host, the last tile in the bottom bar is a dashed one with a
big plus on it. Tap it and it flips over to two buttons, **Player Invite
Link** and **Viewer Invite Link**; either copies that link to your clipboard,
ready to send. Each is one link for the whole session: send the same one to
everyone you want in, and each person who opens it gets their own seat.
Links stop working when the session ends.

<Callout type="info">
In a solo session, chat and webcam are hidden until someone else joins, so a
single-player game doesn't clutter the screen with controls nobody's using.
Opening an invite link always shows the full room, even into a session that
started solo.
</Callout>

## The bottom bar

Everything but the game itself lives in the bar along the bottom (swipe it
down, or tap the handle at the bottom edge, to hide it and get the whole
screen back). Left to right:

- **The stream controls** at the left edge: a volume fader for the game with
  a mute button under it, a **Reload Stream** button for the rare case the
  stream freezes, and **Gaming Mode**, described below.
- **The controller tray** (host only), then **your own tile**, then a tile
  for everyone else in the room.
- **The invite tile** (host only), described above.
- **Chat**, on the right: the most recent messages with a box to type into.
  Tap into the box and it grows up over the bottom half of the game to show
  the whole history; tap anywhere else (or press Esc) and it drops back
  down. While it's closed, new messages pop up briefly above it so you don't
  miss them. On a small screen, once there are enough tiles to need the
  room, chat folds to a narrow tab at the right edge with a dot for unread
  messages; tap the tab to open it.

Saving and stopping the game are RomM's own controls, on the bar RomM shows
above the stream, not the room's. Volume appears in both places: the room's
fader is the only one an invite link gets, and inside RomM it sits alongside
RomM's, each turning the same game down.

### Gaming mode

The crosshair button blows the stream up to the whole screen and hands the
game your mouse the way a native game takes it: the pointer stops being a
cursor on a page and becomes aim, so mouselook works instead of the mouse
sliding off the edge of the stream. It only appears while you're the one
holding mouse and keyboard, since there's nothing to aim with otherwise.

Press **Esc three times quickly** to come back out. The first two go through
to the game, so in-game menus still open on them.

<Callout type="warn" title="Esc may drop you out on the first press.">
Trapping Esc needs a keyboard lock that browsers only grant to the page
you're actually looking at. On an invite link that's the room, so the three
press exit works. Inside RomM the room is in a frame and the lock is RomM's
to ask for, so the first Esc leaves gaming mode. Mouselook still works
either way.
</Callout>

### Your own tile

Hover over your own video (or tap it on a touch screen) and three buttons
appear over it: microphone on/off, webcam on/off, and a settings gear. They
only show while you're pointing at the tile, so the rest of the time it's
just your video.

The gear opens a small dialog for picking which microphone and camera to
use, if your device has more than one (your choice is remembered for next
time).

Spectators have no tile of their own and so none of these, since there's
nothing for them to control.

## Chat and webcam

Chat is plain text, with a reply button on each message if you want to quote
someone. Joins, leaves, and username changes show up as system messages so
the room stays easy to follow.

Turning your webcam or mic on shares them with everyone else in the room, not
just the host. Each person's video tile has its own mute buttons so anyone
can locally mute someone else's audio or hide their video without affecting
what that person is actually sending. The host also has a star icon on each
tile to highlight one person as the designated speaker, a visual cue for the
room rather than anything that changes audio.

<Callout type="warn" title="Your browser will ask for camera/mic permission the first time.">
If it's ever denied by mistake, the button stays available: click it again
and your browser will offer to re-ask.
</Callout>

## Getting a controller

This is the room's signature feature: the host can hand a virtual gamepad, or
the mouse and keyboard, to anyone in the room, live, without pausing the
game.

Only the host sees the tray of unassigned controller icons (gamepad icons
numbered by slot, plus one mouse-and-keyboard icon). The host drags an icon
from the tray onto someone's video tile to hand them that input; dragging it
back to the tray takes it away again. Whoever currently holds a gamepad slot
or the mouse and keyboard sees that icon sitting on their own tile, so
everyone in the room can see at a glance who's driving.

<Steps>
<Step>
### The host drags a controller icon

Any of the numbered gamepad icons, or the mouse-and-keyboard icon, from the
tray onto a participant's tile (or back onto the host's own tile).
</Step>
<Step>
### The recipient's icon appears on their tile

It moves live, with no reload, so everyone can see who currently has which
input.
</Step>
<Step>
### That person's gamepad or mouse/keyboard now drives the game

Only while they hold the icon. Taking it back (or handing it to someone else)
is instant.
</Step>
</Steps>

Spectators are never a valid drop target: the host can watch and chat with
them, but can't hand them a controller.

## Resolution and stream layout

The game stream automatically resizes to fit whoever's watching. If you're
the host, a lock icon on your own tile freezes the current resolution so it
stops adjusting itself as people join and leave; unlocking it goes back to
automatic. A second icon lets the host manually match the stream's resolution
to one specific participant's screen.

Video tiles (yours and everyone else's) can be dragged to reorder them in the
grid, and the whole video row can be collapsed with its own toggle if you'd
rather focus on the game.

## Ending the session

Only the host can end the session, with the stop button on RomM's own bar
above the stream. It ends the session for everyone: the game saves, the
emulator stops, and every link into the room stops working. Guests who
leave (close the tab, lose signal) keep their seat and can come back on the
same link; any controller they were holding goes back to the tray.

## Language

The room's own text (buttons, chat, settings) follows your browser's
language automatically; there's no in-room language switcher, so setting your
browser's preferred language is what changes it. It supports English,
Spanish, Chinese, Hindi, Portuguese, French, Russian, German, Turkish,
Italian, Dutch, Arabic, Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese, Thai, Filipino, and
Danish.
